## A. The Irish care pathway

### Who to see for what

| ID | Finding | Source |
|----|---------|--------|
| D1 | HSE guidance for GPs: telling dental from non-dental pain "can be difficult"; **acute dental pain needs a dental opinion because procedures, not prescriptions, fix it**; facial or intra-oral swelling needs an *urgent* dental opinion. A GP can bridge with pain relief but is not the fix. | [HSE, Advice for GPs on atraumatic dental pain](https://assets.hse.ie/media/documents/Advice_for_general_medical_practitioners_on_the_management_of_atraumatic_dental_pain.pdf) |
| D2 | Toothache can be mimicked by sinusitis, TMJ disorders, primary headaches and neuropathies, and vice versa. Dentists often know little about headache; physicians often don't know the varied presentations of toothache. | [Headache review, PubMed 31675112](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31675112/) |
| D3 | Adults with a **medical card** get dental care through the DTSS (exam, cleaning, extractions, two fillings a year) from participating private dentists; some counties have none. Patients must say "medical card" when booking. The Irish Dental Association has called DTSS effectively emergency-only (2018). | [HSE dental](https://hse.ie/eng/services/list/2/dental); [HSE GP guidance](https://assets.hse.ie/media/documents/Advice_for_general_medical_practitioners_on_the_management_of_atraumatic_dental_pain.pdf); [IDA](https://dentist.ie/dentists-say-its-time-to-consign-unfit-for-purpose-medical-card-scheme-to-history-new-president-of-irish-dental-association-says-revamped-prsi-scheme-shows-what-can-be-achie/) |
| D4 | Adults **without** a medical card use private dentists. Workers with enough PRSI contributions get a **free annual check-up** and a subsidised scale & polish (Treatment Benefit). The dentist can check eligibility. | [Smiles, dental benefits](https://www.smiles.ie/smiles-dental-price-list/dental-benefits/); [IDA](https://dentist.ie/70-of-prsi-payers-missed-free-dental-check-up-last-year-irish-dental-association/) |
| D5 | **~70% of eligible PRSI payers didn't use the free check-up** (2.25m eligible, 687k used it, 2021). People don't know what they're entitled to. | [Irish Dental Association](https://dentist.ie/70-of-prsi-payers-missed-free-dental-check-up-last-year-irish-dental-association/) |
| D6 | Out-of-hours dental care is thin. Dublin Dental Hospital runs a weekend/bank-holiday line for **severe** emergencies only (01 612 7200); otherwise the ED. In Dec 2024 SouthDoc's medical director said there were **no public or private out-of-hours dental services in Cork and Kerry**. | [HSE Dublin South City dental](https://hse.ie/eng/services/list/1/lho/dubsouthcity/dental-and-orthodontic-services); [Medical Independent](https://www.medicalindependent.ie/2024/12/16/concerns-over-lack-of-ooh-dental-care-services/) |
| D7 | When a GP is closed, regional **out-of-hours GP co-ops** cover (e.g. SouthDoc 0818 355 999 in Cork/Kerry, Caredoc in the south-east, MIDOC in the midlands, appointment-only). **Injury units** handle recent non-life-threatening injuries and are often faster than EDs. **EDs** are for emergencies; 112/999 when serious. Numbers and hours vary by region. | [HSE Cork](https://about.hse.ie/news/injury-units-and-hospital-alternatives-in-cork/); [HSE Kerry](https://about.hse.ie/news/caring-for-kerry-your-alternatives-to-busy-emergency-departments/); [Midlands 103](https://midlands103.com/news/midlands-news/hse-advising-people-not-to-get-caught-on-the-hop-with-minor-injuries) |
| D8 | **Pharmacists can now prescribe** for eight common conditions under the Common Conditions Service: hay fever, cold sores, conjunctivitis, oral thrush, shingles, impetigo, uncomplicated UTI, vulvovaginal thrush. Regulations signed Nov 2025; pharmacies to deliver by 31 March 2026. Several of these are women-heavy conditions. | [Law Society Gazette](https://www.lawsociety.ie/gazette/top-stories/2025/november/pharmacists-may-now-prescribe-for-certain-conditions/); [PSI](https://www.psi.ie/information-and-resources/common-conditions-service) |
| D9 | **HSE Live** (1800 700 700, Mon–Fri 8–8, Sat 9–5) answers questions about services and entitlements. It is not for emergencies. | [HSE contact](https://www2.hse.ie/services/contact-the-hse/) |

## B. Women's health context

| ID | Finding | Source |
|----|---------|--------|
| D15 | Women in EDs with the same pain scores as men were **13–25% less likely to get opioid analgesia** and waited longer for it (65 vs 49 min median). | [Chen et al. 2008, Acad Emerg Med](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18439195/) |
| D16 | In a large ED dataset, women were less likely to receive analgesics at every pain score and age, and their pain score was less likely to be recorded at triage. | [King's College London](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/news/study-finds-women-less-likely-to-be-prescribed-pain-relief-than-men) |
| D17 | **Endometriosis takes 7–10 years to diagnose** in Ireland according to advocates and clinicians; there are no official Irish statistics. A National Framework now sets a care pathway, but hubs are reported under-staffed. | [NWCI, 2026](https://www.nwci.ie/news/blog-article/corrinne_hasson_women_are_waiting_nine_years_for_endometriosis_diagnosis); [Farmers Journal](https://www.farmersjournal.ie/life/health/endometriosis-in-ireland-we-can-t-stop-fighting-for-better-care-908501) |
| D18 | Ireland's **Women's Health Action Plan 2024–2025 (Phase 2)** expands specialist menopause clinics, See-and-Treat gynaecology clinics and free contraception (€11m+, €140m+ since 2020). | [gov.ie](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-health/publications/womens-health-action-plan-2024-2025-phase-2-an-evolution-in-womens-health) |
| D19 | The **Free Contraception Scheme** covers ages 17–35, covers GP consultations and prescriptions, needs no medical card, and is open to women, trans and non-binary people for whom it's suitable. | [HSE free contraception](https://www2.hse.ie/services/sexual-health/free-contraception/); [PQ 37300/24](https://www.hse.ie/eng/about/personalpq/pq/2024-pq-responses/september-2024/pq-37300-24-sean-sherlock.pdf) |

**Implication:** a log of symptoms over time, in the user's own words, is evidence. It helps someone be believed and shortens the road to diagnosis. `[assumption grounded in D15–D17]`

## C. Memory, recall and health literacy

| ID | Finding | Source |
|----|---------|--------|
| D20 | Patients forget **40–80%** of medical information immediately; nearly half of what they recall is wrong. More information = more forgotten; anxiety makes it worse. | [Kessels 2003](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC539473/) |
| D21 | Giving patients recordings of consultations improves **recall, satisfaction and decision-making**; ~72% listen back; ~80% rate them useful. Patients also report more trust in the clinician. | [Scoping review, PMC9836232](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9836232/); [Liddell et al., BJGP 2004](https://bjgp.org/content/bjgp/54/506/667.full.pdf); [Breast reconstruction study](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8571820/) |
| D22 | **28–40% of people in Ireland have limited health literacy**, with most difficulty in *finding and evaluating* health information. | [NALA 2012 report](https://www.nala.ie/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/Health-literacy-in-Ireland-2012.pdf); [NALA](https://www.nala.ie/?p=1603) |
| D23 | The HSE itself encourages people to keep a medicines list to show at pharmacy/hospital visits. | [HSE news](https://about.hse.ie/news/hse-encourages-people-to-use-a-medicines-list/) |

## D. AI symptom checkers: accuracy and trust

| ID | Finding | Source |
|----|---------|--------|
| D24 | Across 23 symptom checkers, the correct diagnosis came first only **34%** of the time; triage advice was appropriate **57%** overall (80% emergencies, 33% self-care). Tools were **risk-averse**, over-sending people to care. (2015, tools have improved since.) | [Semigran et al., BMJ 2015](https://www.bmj.com/node/900623) |
| D25 | Symptom-checker accuracy has improved with newer tools, but authors still frame them as a complement to, not a replacement for, a clinician. | [Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/quality/hospital-physician-relationships/symptom-checkers-demonstrate-increasing-accuracy/) |
| D26 | Classification follows **intended purpose**, including marketing. An app that starts deciding urgency can move into medical-device territory; AI in such devices adds AI Act obligations (data governance, logging, human oversight). | [Validaitor](https://www.validaitor.com/post/healthcare-ai-under-the-eu-ai-act); [Reed Smith](https://reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102kq35/the-eu-ai-act-and-medical-devices-navigating-high-risk-compliance) |

**Implication:** the AI's value is in **asking good questions, structuring the story and pointing to the right door**, not naming a disease. That is both safer and closer to what the founding story needed.

| ID | Finding | Source |

## Summary: what the evidence says

1. **The Irish system is navigable but opaque.** The right door depends on symptom × entitlements × time × place (D1–D14). Most people don't know their entitlements (D5).
2. **Toothache is a perfect example**: the "obvious" door (GP) is often the wrong one (D1), out-of-hours dental is a gap (D6), and cost decides behaviour (D10–D11).
3. **People forget most of what they're told** (D20), and having a record helps (D21, D23).
4. **Women's pain is under-treated and under-believed** (D15–D17). A timeline of their own words helps.
5. **AI should navigate, not diagnose** (D24–D26).
6. **Privacy is the price of entry** (D27–D28).
7. **Inclusion is anatomy-first language** (D29–D30).
