## 1 · Ana (primary)
**28 · she/her · Dublin · moved to Ireland 2 years ago · works full-time, PRSI-paying, no medical card**

*Based on the founding story.*

**Situation:** A molar has ached on and off for a week; now it throbs at night and the pain reaches her ear. She isn't registered with a GP yet because the two near her said their lists were closed (D13). She has never used an Irish dentist.

**Goals**
- Find out if this is urgent and who actually fixes it.
- Not waste €50–€60 on the wrong appointment (D10).
- Remember what the dentist says and show it if it comes back.

**Frustrations**
- Google gives her NHS pages and US prices.
- She didn't know about the free PRSI dental check-up (D5).
- After appointments she remembers "something about antibiotics and come back in two weeks" (D20).

**Context of use:** Late evening on the sofa in pain, one hand on her jaw. Waiting rooms. Walking out of an appointment.

**Tech:** iPhone, uses voice notes a lot, trusts apps that look calm and "official-ish".

> "I just want someone to tell me: is this a dentist thing or a doctor thing, and is it tonight or tomorrow?"

**Design implications:** voice-first intake; face/jaw map; Irish routing with costs; post-visit voice recap; follow-up reminder.

## 2 · Siobhán (secondary: the long story)
**42 · she/her · Kilkenny · two kids · medical card: no, GP visit card: no · symptoms for 3+ years**

**Situation:** Heavy, painful periods, fatigue, brain fog, sleep disruption. Has seen three GPs and a gynaecologist; told "it's stress" and "it might be perimenopause". Each new clinician starts from scratch (D15–D17).

**Goals**
- Have a single timeline showing how long this has gone on and what's been tried.
- Be taken seriously.
- Know about menopause clinics and See-and-Treat gynae clinics (D18).

**Frustrations**
- Notes are scattered across a paper diary, texts and screenshots.
- She freezes in appointments and forgets her list.

**Context of use:** Quick daily check-ins; a prep session the night before an appointment; showing her phone across the desk.

> "If I could just hand them three years of my life on one screen, they'd stop telling me it's stress."

**Design implications:** timeline with patterns; "prepare for appointment" mode that builds a question list; show-doctor view with duration and what's been tried.

## 3 · Rory (inclusive edge case)
**24 · they/them · Galway · student · non-binary, assigned female at birth · GP visit card via income**

**Situation:** Recurring pelvic pain and a UTI-like burn. Avoids GPs after being misgendered and asked intrusive questions (D29–D30). Unaware pharmacists can now treat uncomplicated UTIs (D8).

**Goals**
- Describe their body without being forced into "woman" or "man".
- Find the lowest-friction, most respectful route.
- Keep control of their data.

**Frustrations**
- Apps that ask "Are you male or female?" at sign-up.
- Body maps that are only one shape.

> "Ask me about my body, not my gender. And don't sell my data."

**Design implications:** anatomy-first optional questions; neutral body map; name and pronouns on the show-doctor view; local-first storage and clear privacy copy (D27–D28).
